## Free shipping is the default here, not the bait

Zappos ships free anywhere in the United States, including US territories and military APO/FPO addresses. No minimum order, no membership required. Standard delivery runs 3–5 business days.

Expedited shipping (1–3 business days) is the part that's tied to membership: it's free for VIP members. There's one geographic catch — expedited isn't available outside the continental US or to APO/FPO addresses, so if you're ordering to Hawaii, Guam or a military address, plan around standard or USPS delivery.

International shipping isn't part of the deal. If you're outside the US, Zappos isn't your retailer unless you're using a forwarding service, and free returns stop at the US border.

## The return window, in plain numbers

This is where a lot of outdated blog posts get it wrong. The version of the return policy that circulates most widely says "365-day returns." Zappos' own shipping and returns page, last updated in July 2025, splits it differently:

- Returns dropped off and scanned within **60 days** of purchase: refund to your original payment method, or store credit if you prefer.
- Past 60 days: eligible items can still go back as **store credit for up to one year** from the purchase date.
- Guest checkout orders: 60 days only, and refunds go back to the original payment method. No store credit option.

Two details people discover at the worst moment. Footwear has to include the original shoebox in its original condition, with no packing tape or postal labels stuck on it. And returns need to be unworn, in original packaging, with tags and any security tags attached. Final Sale items aren't returnable at all.

The refund speed is genuinely faster than most retailers: for eligible orders, the refund is issued once the carrier scans the package at drop-off, not when it lands in the warehouse. Store credit may take up to 24 hours; card and PayPal postings depend on your bank.

Exchanges work differently from most sites. When you start an exchange, the value of the item you're sending back moves into an e-gift card that funds the replacement order immediately — so the new pair ships before your return arrives. The trade-off: the original item has to be back within 14 days, or the card on file gets charged for the replacement.

The exclusion list is where people lose money, so it's worth reading properly:

- **Birkenstock and VRSNL purchases earn nothing.** So do orders flagged as being for resale.

The referral program is the part that's easy to fumble. Three rules that quietly disqualify people:

## Sizing, widths, and why the return policy is the real feature

Zappos' stock skews deep rather than narrow: the same shoe in multiple widths, half sizes, and the kind of extended sizing that makes a difference in athletic styles. Deal listings regularly show X-Wide and EE widths in Nike and New Balance running shoes getting discounted, which is unusual elsewhere — wide widths tend to sit full price while standard sizes get the markdowns.

For kids, listings run across toddler, little kid and big kid sizing in the same catalog, so a single order can cover school shoes and a pair of sneakers without juggling two retailers.

The reason this matters is the 60-day refund window. Ordering two sizes and sending one back costs you nothing in shipping, and if you miss the 60-day mark, unused items can still go back as store credit for up to a year. That's a real buffer for anyone who buys shoes out of season.

Two practical notes. If you want the store-credit option as a fallback, don't check out as a guest. And if you keep the shoebox, the return is straightforward — remove shipping labels, don't tape the box, and use the QR code or printed UPS label from your order history.
